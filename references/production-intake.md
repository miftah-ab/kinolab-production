# Start New Production — Intake

## Interaction rules

- Ask one question per turn. Keep wording plain; explain technical terms only when needed.
- Do not show all questions as a long form.
- Skip anything already answered or clearly visible in supplied assets.
- Offer "You decide" for creative choices. When chosen, make a sensible recommendation and explain it briefly.
- Ask only for information that materially changes the production.
- If the user is unsure, use a reasonable default and mark it as an assumption for approval.
- Distinguish a client commission from a portfolio/spec concept.
- Do not begin expensive generation or detailed production until the brief and concept are approved.

## Intake sequence

Ask in this order, adapting to the answers:

1. **What are we making?** Commercial/ad, product advertisement, restaurant/café promo, hotel/real-estate promo, brand film, event promo, or other.
2. **What format?** Vertical 9:16, horizontal 16:9, square 1:1, or multiple aspect ratios. Explain common destinations only if useful.
3. **Which business/product/service?** Name, description, what it sells, location if relevant, and the exact offer or feature to promote.
4. **What should the video achieve?** Awareness, product introduction, promote an offer, visits, inquiries, bookings, or another measurable action.
5. **Who should care?** Target customer, geography, and relevant customer need or desire.
6. **What assets exist?** Product photos, location footage, logo, brand guide, existing video, testimonials, or no assets. Ask the user to attach what they have.
7. **What creative direction?** Premium/cinematic, energetic, minimal/elegant, emotional/story-driven, documentary, or "you decide".
8. **How long?** Recommend 15–30 seconds for a focused social ad unless the goal needs more time. Options may include 15, 30, 45, 60 seconds or custom.
9. **Voice and sound?** Voiceover, dialogue, music-led, natural sound, or combination; language/accent if voice is required.
10. **Production constraints?** Deadline, budget range (including zero), available tools, footage access, and whether AI visuals, real footage, Blender 3D, or a mix is acceptable.

## Minimum viable brief

Do not force all ten answers if the project is simple. Before concept development, the minimum is:
- Project type and client/spec status.
- Subject/business/product.
- Intended audience or market.
- Objective / desired action.
- Aspect ratio and destination.
- Approximate duration.
- Available assets and important product facts.
- Budget/tool constraints if they affect production.

If something is unknown, mark it as an assumption and ask for approval rather than inventing it.

## Brief confirmation

Summarize the answers in a compact brief:
- Project name and status: client project or spec/portfolio.
- Business/product and verified facts.
- Audience and market.
- Objective and CTA.
- Format, duration and destination.
- Creative direction.
- Available assets.
- Voice and sound preference.
- Budget, deadline and tools.
- Open questions / assumptions.

Ask: "Is this brief correct? Approve it or tell me what to change."

Only proceed after approval. If the user has explicitly said to use judgment and proceed, record that authorization and continue.

## Avoid intake mistakes

- Do not ask the user to choose a camera lens before they have approved a concept.
- Do not ask for a brand guide if the user has no brand assets.
- Do not ask for budget as a gate to free planning; ask because it changes production choices.
- Do not assume every ad needs voiceover, 3D, generated video, or a website CTA.
- Do not use fake reviews, invented product claims, false scarcity, fabricated discounts, or unverified performance promises.
