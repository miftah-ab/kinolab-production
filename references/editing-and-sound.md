# Editing, Motion Graphics, Color and Sound

## Editing plan

Create an edit decision list / timeline with:
- Shot ID.
- In/out time and duration.
- Purpose and visual action.
- Cut type or transition.
- Speed changes, if any.
- Text overlay and exact timing.
- Music, ambience, Foley and SFX cues.
- Notes for crop/reframe in each aspect ratio.

Prefer clean cuts and motivated transitions. Avoid transition effects that distract from the product or make the ad feel templated.

## Editor compatibility

Give editor-neutral instructions first. When the user edits in CapCut Desktop, translate them into practical CapCut steps and avoid paid-only features unless the user explicitly approves them. Do not assume a plugin or feature exists in the user's installed version.

## Motion graphics and typography

- Add precise logos, labels, prices, captions and CTA in post, not in generated frames.
- Keep typography readable on mobile and within platform-safe areas.
- Use a small, consistent type system.
- Motion should support hierarchy and timing, not decorate every shot.
- Confirm spelling, currency, dates, offer terms and CTA with the user.

## Color

Define a look that supports the brand and product. Do not impose a fixed palette on every client. Keep skin, food, materials and product colors credible. Avoid crushing detail, clipping highlights or oversaturating brand colors.

## Sound design is mandatory planning

For each shot, consider:
- Music: emotional role, energy, instrumentation, entry, build, drops and ending.
- Ambience: location bed and environmental realism.
- Foley: tactile product/hand/object sounds synchronized to action.
- SFX: only purposeful emphasis, movement or transition cues.
- Voice/dialogue: exact line, delivery, language, timing and intelligibility.
- Mix: music ducking, voice priority, transient control, headroom and platform-appropriate loudness.

## Sound rules

- Every sound cue must have a reason and a timeline position.
- Do not add random whooshes, risers, impacts or cinematic booms.
- Do not stack competing effects on every cut.
- Use legally usable audio. Check source license, attribution and commercial-use permissions.
- Do not imitate a living artist's voice or imply endorsement.
- If no actual audio file is accessible, provide a cue sheet and mix instructions; do not claim to have listened to it.
- Provide exact relative balance guidance where useful, but do not invent measured loudness values. Measure the final export with an audio meter when available.

## CapCut-friendly handoff

Provide a practical sequence: import assets, set project ratio/frame rate, assemble shot IDs, trim to timeline, add overlays, apply restrained transitions, place music/ambience/Foley/SFX on separate tracks, mix, review on phone-sized preview, export, and run final QA. Avoid instructions that depend on paid CapCut features unless clearly marked optional.
