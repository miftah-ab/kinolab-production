# Production Workflow and Approval Gates

## Stage sequence

1. Intake and business truth.
2. Commercial angle and creative concept.
3. Script / timed beat sheet.
4. Visual bible and reference selection.
5. Storyboard and shot list.
6. Still-frame / previs approval.
7. Cost and generation pilot.
8. Shot production and inspection.
9. Edit, motion graphics and color.
10. Sound design and mix.
11. Technical QA and human review.
12. Delivery and archive.

## Approval gates

- **Gate A — Brief:** Confirm the business, audience, objective, CTA, format, assets and constraints.
- **Gate B — Concept:** Approve the central idea, hook, message and ending before writing detailed prompts.
- **Gate C — Script:** Approve the exact message and timing.
- **Gate D — Look:** Approve visual bible and reference frames before producing many shots.
- **Gate E — Previs:** Approve storyboard and shot order.
- **Gate F — Cost/pilot:** State which tools may incur cost, what one test shot will cost if known, and ask for explicit permission before paid batch generation.
- **Gate G — Assembly:** Review shot quality, edit rhythm and sound before final export.
- **Gate H — Delivery:** Confirm technical specs, rights, file names and final human review.

## Change control

Maintain one source-of-truth brief. When a user changes a decision:
- Identify which downstream deliverables are affected.
- Update only those deliverables.
- Preserve approved decisions that are not affected.
- Record version/date or a concise change note.
- Do not silently change aspect ratio, duration, CTA, brand palette, product appearance or approved script.

## Production status

Use clear statuses: Draft, Awaiting Approval, Approved, In Production, Needs Revision, QA, Delivered. Never label a stage Approved without user approval or explicit authorization.
