# Commercial Script, Storyboard and Shot Coverage

## Script format

For short ads, write a timed audiovisual script rather than prose alone. Each beat includes:
- Timecode and duration.
- Visual action and subject.
- Voiceover/dialogue or on-screen copy.
- Sound/music cue.
- Commercial purpose.
- Transition into next beat.

Read every line aloud or estimate natural delivery time. Do not cram copy beyond the time available. Allow breathing room for product visuals and CTA.

## Script principles

- Start with the most compelling product, problem, transformation, sensory detail or audience-relevant statement.
- Make the value understandable without relying only on cinematic mood.
- Show proof or a concrete demonstration when possible.
- Keep one primary message and one primary CTA.
- Avoid generic superlatives, unsupported comparative claims and empty adjectives.
- Ensure spoken copy and on-screen copy do not compete by repeating the same paragraph unnecessarily.
- Use language natural to the target audience; localize meaning, not just words.

## Storyboard

Create one frame/description per meaningful shot or beat. Include:
- Shot ID.
- Rough frame composition.
- Subject placement and action.
- Camera angle/movement.
- Lighting and background.
- Text-safe area.
- Continuity links.
- Audio intention.
- Shot duration and transition.

For complex sequences, create a simple animatic using stills and timing before expensive generation or full 3D work.

## Coverage and feasibility

For real footage, plan wide, medium, close-up, detail/macro and cutaway coverage only when each shot serves the edit. Specify:
- Location and time of day.
- Required people/props/products.
- Action and blocking.
- Camera support and movement.
- Lighting/control needs.
- Ambient sound and clean audio takes.
- Permissions, safety and weather/contingency.

Do not assume one generated clip can deliver several precise actions or camera moves reliably. Break complex choreography into simple shots.
