# End-to-End Commercial Production Workflow

## Stages

1. Intake, client status, business truth and rights.
2. Audience insight, objective and creative angle.
3. Concept options and recommendation.
4. Script, timed beats and CTA.
5. Visual bible, reference research and asset plan.
6. Storyboard, animatic/previs and shot list.
7. Production method selection: live action, licensed stock, image/video generation, Blender 3D, or hybrid.
8. Still/keyframe approval and one-shot pilot.
9. Shot production, review and targeted fixes.
10. Edit assembly, motion graphics, captions and color.
11. Voice, music, ambience, Foley, SFX and mix.
12. Platform versions, captions and exports.
13. Rights/compliance review, technical QC and human review.
14. Client approval, delivery and archive.

## Gates

- **A — Brief:** business facts, audience, objective, CTA, platform, format, assets, budget, permissions.
- **B — Concept:** hook, angle, message and CTA.
- **C — Script:** exact words and duration.
- **D — Look:** visual bible, references and product fidelity.
- **E — Previs:** storyboard, coverage and timing.
- **F — Pilot/cost:** inspect a representative still/shot; get explicit approval before paid or bulk generation.
- **G — Edit/audio:** review rough cut, typography, color, music and sound design.
- **H — Delivery:** rights, final specs, human review and client sign-off.

## Change management

Keep the approved brief as source of truth. When a change arrives:
- Identify affected shots, assets, prompts, edit timings, sound cues and exports.
- Preserve unaffected approved work.
- Update versions and change log.
- Recalculate cost/time when the change materially expands scope.
- Never silently alter product facts, CTA, approved script, aspect ratio or brand identity.

## Status vocabulary

Draft → Awaiting Approval → Approved → In Production → Needs Revision → QA → Delivered. Use "Approved" only after explicit approval. Use "Delivered" only after the files have actually been handed over.
