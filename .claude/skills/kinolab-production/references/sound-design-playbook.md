# Sound Design Playbook

Sound must support the commercial message, physical world and edit rhythm. Do not add effects merely to make every cut feel dramatic.

## Build the audio world
Identify the environment and sound perspective: intimate studio, busy café, open street, luxury hotel, product tabletop, outdoors, etc. Choose whether the sound is realistic, stylized or hybrid. Keep ambience consistent across cuts unless the location changes.

## Music direction
For each music cue define:
- Emotional purpose and energy arc.
- Genre/instrumentation and texture.
- Tempo/rhythm and whether cuts should follow beats.
- Entry point, build, drop, break, ending and fade.
- Space left for voice, product Foley and key SFX.
- Source, licence, commercial use and attribution.

Do not prescribe a copyrighted song for commercial use unless the user has appropriate rights. If the user wants a music-generation prompt, specify original composition characteristics, structure, duration, instrumentation and ending; do not request imitation of a living artist.

## Voice and dialogue
Specify exact approved words, language, accent, voice character, pace, emotional intention, pauses, emphasis and pronunciation guide for names/brands. Keep voice natural and intelligible. Do not add filler copy to fill time. Confirm consent before cloning a real person's voice. Keep a clean dry voice master before processing where possible.

## Foley
Foley should synchronize to visible physical actions: cup placement, liquid pour, package opening, fabric movement, footsteps, cutlery, switch click, product handling. Match perspective, surface, material, distance and room acoustics. Do not add a pour sound when no pour is visible.

## Sound effects
Use SFX for a specific reason: reveal, transition, impact, interface action, movement or emphasis. Design the cue with attack, decay, pitch, texture, distance and stereo position. Avoid stacking multiple impacts/whooshes on every cut. Silence or a short drop in music can be more effective.

## Transition sound
Make audio transitions motivated by picture or story: carry ambience across a cut, use a pre-lap to introduce the next location, let a Foley sound bridge a match cut, or use a restrained riser for a real build. Avoid default whoosh on every transition.

## Mix and timing
- Build separate tracks/buses for voice, music, ambience, Foley and SFX.
- Set gain staging before loudness processing.
- Automate music ducking around speech and important tactile sounds.
- Use EQ to reduce masking, not to create an artificial preset sound.
- Control dynamics without crushing transients.
- Check mono compatibility, stereo balance, fades, phase and clipping.
- Measure integrated loudness and true peak when tools are available; use destination-specific requirements, not a universal LUFS target.

## Cue sheet minimum
Every cue needs cue ID, shot/timecode, layer, description, source/file, purpose, in/out/fade, mix/automation notes, licence and approval status.

## Final listen
Listen to the full cut on headphones and a phone speaker. Also check at low playback volume: the message and CTA should remain understandable. Verify no accidental silence, clicks, abrupt tails or distracting effects.
