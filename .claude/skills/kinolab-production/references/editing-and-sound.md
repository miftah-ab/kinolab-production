# Editing, Motion Graphics, Color and Sound

## Edit decision list

For every shot, define timeline in/out, duration, purpose, action, transition/cut, speed change, text overlay and timing, audio cues, and crop/reframe notes for each deliverable ratio.

Prefer motivated cuts. A transition should communicate time, space, energy or a deliberate brand style. Avoid preset-heavy edits that distract from the product.

## Assembly and pacing

- Start with a meaningful visual or message; avoid long logo intros.
- Remove shots that add no new information, emotion, proof or progression.
- Match cuts to action, sound, shape, screen direction or music only when motivated.
- Check rhythm without music as well as with music; the story must remain clear.
- Keep CTA long enough to read and act on.

## Typography and motion

Add precise logos, prices, captions, labels and CTA in post. Use a coherent type hierarchy, readable sizes, safe placement and restrained animation. Verify spelling, currency, language, prices and offer terms. Never invent font availability or rely on a paid feature without approval.

## Color pipeline

Identify source color space and output color space. Keep exposure and white balance consistent. Match shots before applying a creative look. Preserve skin, food and product color. Avoid clipping highlights, crushed shadows, oversaturation and mixed HDR/SDR errors. Do not assume a LUT is universal across cameras or color spaces.

## Sound design: full-stack approach

Plan separate layers:
1. Dialogue/voiceover: exact words, pronunciation, delivery, pauses, recording/TTS source and processing.
2. Music: emotional role, genre/instrumentation, energy arc, start/end, transitions and license.
3. Ambience: location tone, room tone and environmental bed.
4. Foley: tactile synchronized product/hand/object sounds.
5. SFX: purposeful emphasis, movement and transitions.
6. Mix: gain staging, EQ, dynamics, ducking, stereo placement, fades, headroom and measured loudness/true peak appropriate to destination.

Every cue gets a cue ID, timecode, source, purpose, duration, transition and rights status. No random whooshes, risers, impacts or over-layering.

## Loudness discipline

Do not impose one universal LUFS target on every social ad. Broadcast standards such as EBU R 128 use a different programme-loudness target and are not automatically the target for social platforms. Check the destination's current delivery specification; otherwise avoid destructive loudness normalization, preserve headroom, prevent clipping and report measured integrated loudness/true peak without pretending there is a platform-mandated value.

## Voice and music rights

Do not clone a real person's voice without authorization. Confirm commercial-use and advertising rights for generated voice, music, stock, SFX and fonts. Keep source/licence records in the asset manifest.

## CapCut Desktop handoff

Provide exact, practical steps that work in the user's installed version: project ratio/frame rate, import/organize, assemble by shot ID, trims, overlays, transitions, audio tracks, mix, review and export. Mark paid-only features as optional and never assume the UI/feature is unchanged. Give editor-neutral instructions if the user's version is unknown.
